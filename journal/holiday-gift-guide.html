Gift Guide

The best office gift this year might fit in the palm of your hand

5 min read  ·  Published October 2026

Every December the same four gifts change hands at work: the gift card, the candle, the mug, the logo fleece. Nobody dislikes them. Nobody remembers who gave them, either.

Budgets are tight this year too. Deloitte’s 2025 holiday retail survey found shoppers planning to spend about 10% less than the year before, with roughly three in four planning to trade down on brands or retailers. Spending less makes a generic gift easier to justify. It also makes the gift people do remember stand out more.

What makes a small gift land

The gifts people keep tend to have three things in common. Someone chose them, rather than grabbing the first thing on the shelf. They were easy to receive: no size to guess, no allergy, no wall to hang. And they come with a story the recipient can repeat when someone asks about it.

A Minicuration card covers all three. Each one reproduces an original JFeelgood painting at 2.5 × 3.5 inches, trading card size. Limited editions are numbered out of 50, never reprinted, and arrive sealed in a magnetic acrylic case with a stand, ready for a desk. The original six cost $10 (Sweet Dreams is $8), and the other limited editions are $7.50 on pre-order. Picking a painting for someone tells them you thought about their taste, which a $25 gift card can’t.

Five people on your list who would want one

01The coworker with a curated desk

Some desks have a plant, a good pen and nothing else, on purpose. Those people don’t want clutter; they want one object worth looking at. A cased card stands on its own easel and takes up less room than a phone.

Try Sky Miles: a chrome airplane model set into swirls of blue and white paint, finished with epoxy resin. In stock at $10. It suits the colleague who is always on a plane.

02The client or mentor you want to thank

Gift baskets get shared around the office and forgotten by Friday. A card with the artist’s statement printed on the back gives the person something to read, and a reason to remember who sent it. A small gift also keeps you clear of most workplace gift limits; check your own company’s policy before you buy.

Try Pride, whose statement reads: “Work, no matter the urgency or size, has purpose. If I do not enjoy it, then the effort means nothing and I miss out on life.” $7.50 on pre-order.

03The creative friend

Designers, writers and musicians tend to own plenty of art supplies and very little art by people they could actually meet. A numbered edition of a painting by an independent artist, with materials listed on the back, is the kind of thing they’ll turn over and study.

Try Vicarious, painted in acrylic and latex on torn canvas: “Living in the throes of imagination, I am aged by experiences of a thousand lifetimes…” $7.50 on pre-order.

04The new renter or homeowner

New places have bare walls, and the person living there hasn’t decided what goes on them yet. Large art is a commitment, and a renter may not be allowed to put nails in the wall. A card goes on a shelf or windowsill and moves when they do.

Try Affordable Housing, a small tent beside a tall building, captioned “We can all go outside.” An unlimited card at $4.50 on pre-order; add a case for $4 and a stand for $1.

05The collector who has everything

Collectors are hard to buy for because they already own what they want. A numbered edition of 50 that closes for good once it sells out is something they can’t already have. It also fits the storage they already own: any standard sleeve, toploader or binder page.

Try Poetry of Solace, from the series “The Lost”: “All suffering has cracks and crevices where life grows new…” $7.50 on pre-order.

Buying for a whole team

Each order can hold one card of each limited design, so a team gift is a chance to give everyone a different painting and let them trade. For bigger groups, unlimited cards have no limit, and any 10 cost $37.50 on pre-order.

Timing and logistics

The original six limited editions are in stock and ship within 5–7 business days. Every other card is a pre-order that ships on or before November 1, 2026, well before the holidays. You can cancel a pre-order for a full refund any time before it ships.

Checkout asks for a shipping address, so you can send an order straight to the recipient. Each order goes to one address, and Minicuration ships to US addresses only.

When it’s the wrong gift

Skip it for someone who has told you they don’t want more things on their desk; a card is small, but it’s still a thing. Skip it for anyone outside the US, since it can’t ship there. And be clear about what it is: a reproduction of a painting at card size, not the painting itself. The person who wants an original canvas should look at jfeelgood.com.
